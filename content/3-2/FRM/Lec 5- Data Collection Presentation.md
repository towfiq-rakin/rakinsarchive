
### **Data vs. Information**  
Data represents a set of random, unorganized values, quantities, or figures that may not carry useful meaning until processed. Information is processed and organized data presented within a given context to provide logical meaning, reduce uncertainty, and influence decisions.

|**Feature**|**Data**|**Information**|
|---|---|---|
|**Structure**|Random, unorganized values, quantities, or figures.|Processed, organized, and carries clear meaning.|
|**Origin**|Obtained from research, experiments, or observation.|Obtained directly from data analysis.|
|**Dependency**|Entirely independent of information.|Completely dependent on underlying data.|
|**Utility**|All data may not be useful.|All information is useful and specific in nature.|
|**Workflow**|Acts as the input phase.|Acts as the final output phase.|

**Foundations of Data Collection**  
Data collection is the systematic process of gathering, measuring, and recording raw facts or observations to test hypotheses and support evidence-based choices rather than relying on intuition.
* **Qualitative Data (Attributes):** Characteristics for which numerical values cannot be assigned, such as honesty, motivation, confidence, or integrity.
* **Quantitative Data (Variables):** Characteristics for which a distinct numerical value can be assigned, such as height, weight, or achievement scores.

### **Primary vs. Secondary Data**

**Primary Data**  
This tracks original, first-hand raw research data collected directly by the investigator without any prior processing. 
* **Core Sources:** Empirical laboratory experiments with strict control over external variables , field surveys (sample or census) , questionnaires distributed to scattered sources , face-to-face interviews , and direct observations of natural or controlled behaviors.

**Secondary Data**  
This tracks information compiled from pre-existing research works or public records, used because published statistics are readily available across diverse fields. 
* **Core Sources:** Published printed materials (books, journals, magazines, newspapers) , electronic platforms (e-journals, general websites) , and unpublished personal or government records.

> [!tip] **Secondary Data Evaluation Matrix** 
> **Advantages of Secondary Data**
> * Provides a rapid insight into the total situation and helps unfold hidden information. 
> * Assists significantly in the formulation and testing of research hypotheses. 
> * Provides supplementary or checking information gathered for specific investigations.
> 
> **Disadvantages of Secondary Data** 
> * Prone to deliberate or unintentional bias because it was collected for a specific purpose. 
> * Consists of old data that may lose its timeliness or contextual validity as situations change. 
> * Available in an aggregated form where the unit of aggregation may be inappropriate, or its authenticity is doubtful.

### **Constants, Variables, and Variates**

**Constant**  
A characteristic or condition that remains completely uniform across all observations or sample subjects so it does not interfere with the tested variable. Common constants include fixed room temperatures, matching quantities of substances, identical time durations, and uniform equipment or lighting.

**Variable**  
Behavioral or systemic characteristics that fluctuate from person to person and can be quantified using a measuring instrument.  
* **Continuous Variables:** Quantities for which fractional or decimal values exist and carry logical meaning within the precision of the instrument, such as age (14.5 years) or weight (62.75 kg). 
* **Discrete Variables:** Quantities that exist strictly in non-fractional, whole integer units, such as counts of people (30 boys, 25 girls).

**Variates**  
The actual numerical values collected when an abstract variable is quantified using an instrument. For example, "intelligence" is a variable, an IQ test is the instrument, and the resulting scores (95, 102, 110) are the variates used in statistical analysis.

### Levels (Scales) of Data Measurement

**Nominal Scale (Classification Only):** The most basic level of measurement that groups items into discrete categories without any inherent sequence, priority, or ranking order, such as sorting by gender, pass/fail results, or urban/rural areas.

**Ordinal Scale (Ranking):** Classifies data and arranges it in a comparative order or sequence showing who is higher or lower, although the exact mathematical gaps between ranks are completely unknown and unequal, such as classroom rank positions.

**Equal-Interval Scale (Equal Differences):** Advanced measurement that displays both clear group categorization and exact, meaningful numerical intervals between values, though it lacks an absolute baseline zero, such as standard test scores or temperature in Celsius.

**Ratio Scale (True Measurement):** The most precise level of measurement that includes all features of other scales plus an absolute, true zero point indicating a complete absence of the property. It permits all mathematical operations, making relational comparisons like "twice as much" valid, such as height, weight, distance, and time.

### Dimensions of Graphical Presentation

**One-Dimensional Presentation:** Organizing and displaying data based on a single variable or characteristic at a time without considering relationships with other variables. 
* **Histogram:** Adjacent rectangular bars plotted over continuous data where the horizontal $X$-axis handles class intervals and the vertical $Y$-axis tracks absolute frequency. 
* **Frequency Polygon:** A graph obtained from a histogram by joining the mid-points of the tops of various rectangles with straight lines, adding two arbitrary zero-frequency classes at both boundaries to keep total area equal. 
* **Frequency Curve:** A smooth, continuous version of a frequency polygon that uses a flowing line instead of straight-line segments to represent the overall distribution pattern. 
* **Ogive (Cumulative Frequency Curve):** Represents running totals of frequencies accumulating over class boundaries, where a _less-than ogive_ tracks upper class boundaries upward and a _more-than ogive_ tracks lower class boundaries downward.

> [!note] **Finding the Median Graphically** 
> When both less-than and more-than ogives are plotted on the same graph, the exact point where the two curves intersect projects directly down to provide the median value on the $X$-axis.


* **Pie Chart:** A circular graph representing data as proportions or percentages of a whole, where the entire circle equals 100% (360°) and the angle of each sector is proportional to its frequency or percentage share.

**Two-Dimensional & Three-Dimensional Presentation** 
* **2D Presentation:** Displays data involving two variables simultaneously along two axes (horizontal $X$ and vertical $Y$) to study their direct relationship, such as tracking measured voltage as a function of elapsed time using a scatter plot. 
* **3D Presentation:** Displays three independent variables simultaneously along three spatial axes ($X$, $Y$, and $Z$) within a single figure or model to visualize their combined relationship, such as tracking the composite probability surface of hypertension by age and weight concurrently.
