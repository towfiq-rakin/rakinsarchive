## Question 3
###### a. Name the International Environment Organizations (IEOs). Explain the functions of IEOs on protecting environment. <span style="float: right; ">07</span>
International Environmental Organizations (IEOs) are institutions, either intergovernmental or non-governmental, that work across national borders to address environmental issues. These organizations coordinate global efforts to protect the environment, combat climate change, conserve biodiversity, and promote sustainable development.

**Examples of Prominent IEOs:**
1. UNEP – United Nations Environment Programme 
2. IPCC – Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change
3. IUCN – International Union for Conservation of Nature
4. GEF – Global Environment Facility

**Functions of IEOs in Environmental Governance** 
1. **Facilitating Cooperation:** IEOs promote dialogue and collaboration among nations.
2. **Policy and Norm Setting:** IEOs facilitate international treaties and set environmental standards.
3. **Knowledge Production:** IEOs conduct research and disseminate environmental knowledge.
4. **Capacity Building:** IEOs provide training and technology transfer to countries.
5. **Financial Assistance:** IEOs distribute funds for environmental projects.

###### b. Explain the effect of Rio Earth Summit (1992) and Paris Agreement (2015) on global environment. <span style="float: right; ">08</span>
**Rio Earth Summit**
1. **Agenda 21:** A comprehensive action plan for sustainable development.
2. **Rio Declaration:** 27 principles, including the precautionary principle, polluter pays, and common but differentiated responsibilities (CBDR).
3. **Three major legally binding agreements initiated:**
   - UN Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC)
   - Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD)
   - UN Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD)
1. Also led to the **strengthening of UNEP** and creation of the Commission on Sustainable Development (CSD).  

**Paris Agreement (2015)**
1. **Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs):** Each country sets its own emission targets.
2. **Transparency and Review Mechanisms:** Common framework for reporting progress.
3. **No top-down enforcement**, but relies on transparency, peer pressure, and periodic reviews to encourage compliance.
4. Includes provisions for **climate finance, adaptation, loss and damage**, and **capacity building**.
5. Demonstrated a shift from legally binding emission targets (like in the Kyoto Protocol) to a more **flexible, inclusive model**.
6. Marked a **global political consensus** on the urgency of climate change.

###### a. What are environmental laws? Explain the scopes of environmental laws. <span style="float: right; ">06</span>
Environmental Laws are legal frameworks (treaties, statutes, policies) to protect nature and regulate harmful human activities.  

**Key Purposes:**
1. Prevent pollution & degradation.
2. Conserve biodiversity.
3. Promote sustainability.

**Environmental Law Scope**
1. **Pollution Control:** Air, water, and waste management.
2. **Resource Conservation:** Protecting forests and wildlife habitats.
3. **Climate Change Mitigation:** Reducing emissions, using renewable energy.
4. **Corporate Accountability:** Extended Producer Responsibility, EIA.
###### b. Describe the challenges in Implementation of Environmental Laws. Provide the categories of industries with example according to Department of Environment of Bangladesh. <span style="float: right; ">06</span>
**Challenges in Implementing Laws**
1. **Weak Enforcement**
- **Institutional Deficiencies:** Regulatory bodies often lack the vital funding, staffing, and technical expertise required for consistent monitoring and legal inspections.
- **Corruption & Interference:** Bribery and political influence frequently compromise legal systems, undermining accountability and letting violators bypass penalties.

2. **Conflicting Economic Interests**
- **Short-Term Profit Prioritization:** Economic policies routinely favor immediate financial gains (e.g., from deforestation or mining) over long-term ecological sustainability.
- **Industrial & Labor Pressure:** Powerful corporate lobbying combined with the fear of potential job losses creates heavy resistance to strict regulatory implementation.

3. **Global Inequity**
- **Resource & Technology Gaps:** Developing nations struggle with basic legal implementation due to a lack of access to advanced clean technologies and institutional infrastructure. 
- **Unfair Treaty Burdens:** International agreements often impose uniform compliance standards that poorer nations cannot realistically fulfill without external financial and technical assistance.
###### c. Explain Circular Economy Network. <span style="float: right; ">08</span>
Circular economy is an economic system designed to eliminate waste and continuously reuse resources.
>[!note]- Core Idea
>"Closed-loop" systems where products/materials are recycled, repaired, or repurposed.

![[circular-economy.png]]

1. **Raw materials:** Sourcing and extracting natural inputs efficiently to minimize initial resource depletion.
2. **Sustainable design:** Engineering products from the start for longevity, easy maintenance, and simple recycling.
3. **Production:** Manufacturing goods using clean processes that lower energy use and industrial emissions.
4. **Distribution:** Transporting finished items to markets using eco-friendly and optimized logistics.
5. **Consumption, Reuse, Repair:** Extending product lifespans through active utilization, fixing, and secondhand sharing.
6. **Collection:** Gathering discarded products systematically from consumers to keep them out of landfills.
7. **Residual waste:** Minimizing the final, unavoidable fraction of trash that completely exits the economic loop.
8. **Waste management:** Processing and recycling recovered materials to funnel them back into production as new inputs.

###### a. What is EIA? Describe the key features of EIA.<span style="float: right; ">07</span>
EIA *(Environmental Impact Assessment)* is a process which can be used to improve decision-making and ensure that development options under consideration are environmentally, socially and economically sound and sustainable.  

**Key features of EIA**  
1. EIA is a continuous and integral component of planning that should run continuously throughout the planning cycle of any development initiative.
2. EIA facilitates dialogue, prediction and response, and provides a forum for proponents, decision-makers and the public; to consider the potential impacts of a project on local communities, natural resources and environmental quality.
3. EIA helps – 
   - to promote conservation, 
   - to enhance new economic and social opportunities, and 
   - to introduce long-term environmental protection.
4. EIA provides a framework for stakeholder participation in decision-making that can facilitate public participation within the project cycle and bring various stakeholder groups together and provide an opportunity to exchange information and build consensus between the groups involved.
5. EIA is a tool to improve decision-making, and provides project-specific and strategic information before project implementation decisions are reached.

| **Benefit**                                           | **Description**                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

###### c. Who all are involved in the EIA process?<span style="float: right; ">05</span>
Five principal groups of stakeholders should be involved. These include:
1. **Project Proponents:** Government ministries, private sector companies, or development agencies responsible for commissioning and paying for the EIA process.    
2. **EIA Practitioners:** Individual consultants, academic institutes, and NGOs who actively undertake the assessment and provide technical inputs.
3. **Reviewers:** Academic experts, NGOs, or government officials responsible for quality control, screening, and ensuring the process meets comprehensive terms of reference.
4. **Decision-Makers:** Central or local authorities responsible for making the final decision on project development after reviewing the submitted Environmental Impact Statement (EIS).
5. **The Public:** Local communities and interest groups who provide critical local data, help prevent unforeseen problems, and foster project ownership.
